ChatGPT subscriptions vs. custom AI: when does each win?

Christopher Myers — Founder, Main & Machine · Updated September 2026

Start with the tool you already have. Build only when the workflow needs something it cannot do.

Is a ChatGPT subscription enough for your business?

It may be. Start by testing an approved business tool on the actual task, with the required permissions and a person checking the result. Buy a custom system only when a useful workflow needs connections, controls, or behavior the existing tools cannot provide.

The boundary is changing as subscriptions add apps, connectors, scheduled work, and agents. “A chat window cannot do that” is not a durable buying rule. Check the current product and plan before paying someone to rebuild a capability you already have.

Where should you try the existing tool first?

Try an attended task with a clear input and a reviewable output: a proposal draft, a summary of approved material, an internal checklist, or a response someone will edit before sending. Measure total time, including the check and any corrections.

Business tools may connect to company sources. For example, OpenAI describes business-workspace apps, permissions, and data controls. Availability and behavior depend on the plan and configuration. Review those settings before connecting sensitive material.

When does a workflow need more than a subscription?

When the tested product cannot meet a requirement that matters to the operation. Write the requirement in plain language before deciding what to build:

  • Trigger. What starts the work, and can the existing tool start it reliably?
  • Connections. Can it read and update the required systems with the right permissions?
  • Control. Can you separate routine actions from consequential decisions that need approval?
  • Evidence. Can your team inspect the sources, actions, failures, and review history it needs?
  • Handoff. Can someone maintain it, handle exceptions, and recover when a dependency fails?

A gap may call for a configuration change, an integration, or a defined build. It does not automatically call for a whole AI platform.

What does custom AI cost, and what do you get?

Our AI Implementation Sprint is $18,000–$60,000 over 4 to 12 weeks. The quote fixes the agreed scope before work begins. The deliverable is a working agent, automation, or integration tested in your operation, with documentation, ownership, and team training.

The scope defines what can run automatically and what a person must approve. Custom does not mean infallible or unattended. Budget tools and model usage separately; our typical planning band is $50–$500 a month, excluding hardware, internal time, the build, and optional Managed Services from $1,500 a month.

How do you compare the economics?

Use the actual plan price and the people who need it. For illustration, twenty seats at an assumed $25 a month cost $6,000 a year. That arithmetic is not a current quote for a named product. Check required licenses, billing commitment, usage limits, and taxes.

Then compare the same task under both options. Include setup, review, exceptions, support, and the amount of work actually removed. A cheaper license that leaves the whole handoff manual may be the wrong answer; a custom build for a task the subscription already handles may be wrong too.

How do you avoid buying shelfware?

Give one person one useful job and a short trial. Agree what a satisfactory output looks like, record the time before and after, and ask where corrections are still needed. Expand only when the evidence justifies it. Poor adoption can reveal a weak workflow, unsuitable software, missing permissions, or insufficient support; it does not have one universal cause.
