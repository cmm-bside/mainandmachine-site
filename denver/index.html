The Front Range

What the Denver economy actually runs on.

Three clusters where the paperwork problem is worst, and where we spend most of our time.

Construction and the trades

The Front Range has been building continuously for a decade, and the paper has not kept up with the work. Pay applications, lien waivers across four tiers of sub, prevailing-wage documentation on anything touching public money, submittal logs, OSHA records. Colorado’s trust-fund statute makes the payment chain unforgiving: the documentation either exists on the 25th or the cash moves a month late. That is the cycle we most often get called about.

More on this →

Federal, aerospace and the services belt around them

From Littleton down through Aurora and out toward Colorado Springs sits a dense layer of small firms selling into aerospace and federal programs — engineering services, machining, logistics, compliance support. They inherit their customers’ documentation burden without their customers’ back office. Data that cannot go to a public model is the normal case here rather than the exception, which is the constraint our on-premise architecture was built for.

More on this →

Professional services in the middle

Law, accounting, insurance and advisory firms of twelve to eighty people, concentrated downtown, in the Tech Center and along the Boulder corridor. The same pattern every time: intake that eats a senior person’s morning, work-in-progress that ages because nobody has time to chase it, and three systems that disagree about the status of the same matter.
